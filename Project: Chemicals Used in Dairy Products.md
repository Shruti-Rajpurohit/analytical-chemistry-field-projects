# Chemical Analysis of Milk and Dairy Products

**Type:** Solo Field Project
**Year:** First Year, BSc Chemistry (2021–2022)
**Institution:** Vikram University, Ujjain
**Field Site:** Sanchi Milk Plant, Ratlam
**Supervisor:** Prof. Meenal Gupta

---

## Overview

This project examined the analytical methods used in dairy quality
control, combining an industrial field visit with supervised laboratory
reproduction of all procedures. The objective was to understand how
regulatory nutritional and safety standards for milk and dairy products
are verified through physicochemical analysis.

---

## Field Visit — Sanchi Milk Plant

Sanchi Milk Plant, Ratlam, collects raw milk from 12 surrounding
villages and processes it into a range of dairy products. The visit
covered the complete processing chain from raw milk reception through
pasteurization, standardization, and downstream product manufacturing.

The plant operates an in-house quality control laboratory where incoming
milk and finished products are tested against government-prescribed
nutritional and contaminant thresholds before release.

---

## Analytical Methods Studied and Practiced

### HPLC-MS — Lactose Quantification
High-performance liquid chromatography with mass spectrometry detection
was used to quantify lactose content in milk samples. Sample preparation
involved protein precipitation and filtration prior to injection.

### GC-MS — Fatty Acid Profile Analysis
Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry was used to characterize the fatty
acid composition of dairy matrices. Samples were converted to fatty acid
methyl esters (FAMEs) before analysis. Profiles were compared against
reference standards for adulteration detection.

### MIR Spectroscopy — Compositional Analysis
Mid-infrared spectroscopy provided simultaneous determination of fat,
protein, and lactose percentages in liquid milk. The technique allows
rapid, non-destructive analysis and is standard in industrial dairy QC
workflows.

### ICP-MS — Trace Mineral and Heavy Metal Detection
Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry was used to detect and
quantify trace minerals (calcium, magnesium, zinc) and screen for toxic
heavy metals (lead, cadmium, arsenic) in dairy samples. Results were
verified against FSSAI regulatory limits for food safety compliance.

---

## Laboratory Reproduction

All analytical procedures observed at the plant were subsequently
reproduced in the Vikram University chemistry laboratory under the
supervision of Prof. Meenal Gupta. This included sample preparation,
instrument calibration, data acquisition, and results verification
against regulatory standards.

---

## Documentation

Findings were compiled into a structured technical field report covering:
- Instrument parameters and operating conditions
- Sample preparation procedures
- Raw data tables and analytical results
- Comparison against government nutritional and safety standards
- Observations on industrial QC workflow and laboratory practice

---

## Key Learnings

Working with real dairy matrices rather than prepared laboratory
standards introduced genuine analytical challenges, such as matrix effects,
sample turbidity, and variability across batches. The project provided
practical understanding of how regulatory compliance is verified at the
point of production, and how instrument parameters must be adapted for
complex food matrices.
