## Overview

laboratory spectral analysis to document gold extraction, refining, and
characterization methods. The three sections covered extraction
techniques, industrial purification processes, and independent spectral
analysis experiments conducted in the university laboratory.

## Section B — Industrial Purification (Field Visit)

The project included a visit to a gold purification facility in Ratlam
supplying refined gold to regional jewellers. Purification processes
observed and documented:

### Miller Process
Chlorine gas is passed through molten crude gold. Base metals and silver
form chloride compounds that separate as slag, yielding gold of
approximately 99.5% purity.

### Wohlwill Electrolytic Process
Electrorefining in a hydrochloric acid and gold chloride electrolyte.
The crude gold anode dissolves and pure gold deposits at the cathode,
achieving purity of 99.99%.

### Cupellation
Used for silver-bearing gold alloys. The alloy is melted with lead under
oxidizing conditions; base metals oxidize and are absorbed into the
cupel, leaving a gold-silver bead for further separation.

### Electrorefining
Applied for final-stage purification of high-purity gold, using
controlled current density to achieve consistent deposit quality.

## Section C — Spectral Analysis Experiments

All experiments in this section were conducted independently in the
university laboratory and Masters laboratory under supervisor guidance.

### Experiment 1 — Gold Nanoparticle Synthesis and UV-Vis Characterization

Gold nanoparticles were synthesized by the reduction of chloroauric acid
(HAuCl₄) using sodium citrate as the reducing agent. The formation of
nanoparticles was monitored by UV-Vis spectroscopy.

The surface plasmon resonance absorption peak was recorded and used to
estimate particle size. Shift in peak position with varying
reaction conditions was documented.

### Experiment 2 — AAS Calibration and Unknown Concentration Determination

A calibration curve was constructed using gold standard solutions at:
- 1 ppm
- 2 ppm
- 5 ppm
- 10 ppm

Absorbance was measured for each standard using atomic absorption
spectroscopy. Linearity was verified across the calibration range.
The concentration of an unknown gold solution was then determined from
the curve, with full error analysis calculated.

### Experiment 3 — XRF Analysis

X-ray fluorescence spectroscopy was performed using the department
instrument. Samples analyzed included:
- Gold alloys of varying composition
- Ore samples
- Jewellery pieces of unknown karat
- Gold-coated surfaces

Elemental composition and gold percentage were determined for each
sample type.

### Experiment 4 — Gold Adsorption on Activated Carbon

Gold ion adsorption onto activated carbon was investigated as a model
for cyanidation recovery processes. Gold concentration in solution was
measured by UV-Vis and AAS before and after contact with activated
carbon. Adsorption efficiency was calculated and the results discussed
in the context of industrial carbon-in-pulp recovery methods.

techniques and reinforced the importance of rigorous calibration
protocols and error analysis in quantitative analytical work. The
industrial visit provided direct insight into how laboratory-scale
methods translate to production-scale refining operations.
